# 0001 — Domain selection: nostalgia and reminiscence

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-22
- **Deciders:** Whole team

## Context

We needed a domain for the semester project that fits the Social & Mobile Computing brief — that is, a domain where the *social* dimension is essential rather than incidental, and where mobile technology plausibly changes the experience.

Candidate domains were assessed against three criteria: whether the social dimension is intrinsic, whether we could access real users, and whether the space was already saturated by obvious existing products.

## Decision

We selected **supporting relationships through nostalgia and reminiscence**: how people preserve and revisit memories, and how that affects their relationships and connection to their own past.

The framing is deliberately *shared* rather than individual. Nostalgia is well served for individuals by journalling and photo apps; the collective experience of it is not.

## Rationale

- Three semi-structured interviews (ages 60+, late 20s, early 20s) surfaced six consistent themes, including recreative curation, digital/physical asymmetry and place-triggered recall.
- The literature supports memory as identity-relevant and socially constructed (Bluck et al., 2005; Conway, 2005; Wardell & Palombo, 2024), and documents the felt difference between physical and digital mementoes (Petrelli & Whittaker, 2010).
- A survey of existing products (OpenItLater, Timecapsule.com, Capsuled, PersonalCapsule, Day One) found a consistent pattern: capsules are sealed until a date, but authored and opened by one person.

## Consequences

- Our target audience is defined by *group shape* rather than demographics — groups with a shared endpoint approaching.
- We must test whether groups will genuinely commit to a shared future return date, which is currently our largest untested assumption.
- We carry an open question between a nostalgia framing and a reflective-memory framing; these imply different designs and must be resolved before M2.
