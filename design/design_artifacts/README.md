## Design artifacts from Miro
 
Our Miro board is where we worked out the idea together. These images are exported from it, roughly in the order we made them. Each one shows how research or testing turned into a design decision.
 
Note: the app was called *Launch Sequence* while we built it. It is now called *Space-Cap*.
 
---
 
### 01. Framing: sealing, the time in between, unlocking
 
![Board splitting the idea into sealing memories, the time in between, and unlocking memories, with sticky-note questions](design_artifact/01-framing-sealing-time-unlocking.png)
 
- **What it shows:** our first board of ideas, split into three phases. Under each phase are ideas (a disposable camera, notes, anniversary notifications, puzzles, a Lego-like rebuild, a Minecraft avatar world, geo-guessing) and numbered sticky notes with open questions, such as how to seal media and why a group would sign up.
- **Why we made it:** to break a big idea into phases and list what we didn't know.
- **What it led to:** the three-phase shape that the whole project follows: collect, wait, reopen together.
### 02. Ideas for transforming a shared photo
 
![A photo turned into a caricature and into a Lego-like pixelated picture, next to answered sticky notes](design_artifacts/02-ideation-caricature-and-lego-like.png)
 
- **What it shows:** two ways a photo could change when a capsule is reopened (a caricature, and a pixelated "Lego-like" version), with our answers to the open questions: people sign up to *stay in touch*, random-year alerts, and *painting to unlock*.
- **Why we made it:** to explore how a memory could be rebuilt when it is opened, and to answer the questions from board 01.
- **What it led to:** the idea of unlocking with a joint activity, and the first version of a painting or colouring activity.
### 03. Storyboard: the whole ritual
 
![Nine-panel storyboard from sign-up to reveal and a new cycle, with a written description](design_artifacts/03-storyboard-cyclical-time-capsule.png)
 
- **What it shows:** a nine-panel story of the whole ritual: sign up, private submission, sealing, each person getting a key, years passing, a notification, a group painting activity, the reveal, and a new cycle. A written description sits next to it, with the target audience: friends or family who are parting ways.
- **Why we made it:** to explain the concept as a story and to agree who it is for.
- **What it led to:** our core flow (collect, seal, wait, reunite, reveal) and the idea that the group has to come together to unlock.
### 04. User flow
 
![Flow diagram from sign up to a new capsule, with marked-up notes](design_artifacts/04-user-flow-diagram.png)
 
- **What it shows:** the steps from sign up to memory submission, sealing, receiving a key, a reminder to reconvene, solving a puzzle together, the reveal, and starting the next capsule. The sticky note lists three ways to collect memories: drop in images or videos with a caption, a form that helps people be creative, or an activity that creates memories, like a disposable camera.
- **Why we made it:** to agree the steps after a team discussion, and to mark the parts to work on.
- **What it led to:** the screens we built in our first prototype.
### 05. The colouring puzzle
 
![A hand-drawn picture of four friends at university: plain, partly coloured by the team, and fully coloured](design_artifacts/05-colouring-puzzle-sample.png)
 
- **What it shows:** a hand-drawn picture of four friends in front of a university building, shown plain, coloured in by the team as a test, and fully coloured.
- **Why we made it:** to try out a colouring-in unlock, where each person uses their assigned key colours to colour a plain picture.
- **What it led to:** in testing, people were confused about why colouring a picture should unlock a memory. We dropped it and looked for a better unlock.
### 06. The concept: putting the memory back together
 
![Written concept and decision table for rebuilding a memory piece by piece](design_artifacts/06-unlocking-concept-put-the-memory-back-together.png)
 
- **What it shows:** a written concept where the memory is split into pieces, each person gets one, and the group has to rebuild it together. A decision table compares the Lego-like idea, puzzles and geo-guessing. It ends with what we still had to decide and test.
- **Why we made it:** to choose one clear idea for the unlock out of several.
- **What it led to:** the rule behind every later unlock activity: nobody unlocks alone.
### 07. Another idea we compared: the Living Plant
 
![Eight-panel storyboard of a shared plant that grows as a group adds memories](design_artifacts/07-alternative-living-plant-storyboard.png)
 
- **What it shows:** a storyboard of a shared "Memory Garden". Adding a memory waters a plant, and the plant grows through a bud, flower and fruit.
- **Why we made it:** to compare a very different idea with the time capsule.
- **What it led to:** a clear comparison of options (see 08). We kept the capsule concept.
### 08. Why we compared it with other ideas
 
![Write-up of the Living Plant idea with a table comparing it with other options, and a list of things to decide and test](design_artifacts/08-alternative-living-plant-decision-frames.png)
 
- **What it shows:** the Living Plant explained in detail, a table of options and decisions (a plant grown from a seed, a fixed-slot "developing roll", and an already-alive plant), and a list of things we still had to decide and test.
- **Why we made it:** to make the decision on evidence and to show the reasoning, not just the choice.
- **What it led to:** a record of what we considered. The capsule concept went forward.
### 09. Our first build
 
![Screens from the first prototype: Mission Control, joining on a phone, answering memory questions, engines lighting, and keys assigned](design_artifacts/09-first-build-screens.png)
 
- **What it shows:** the first working prototype, with a shared Mission Control screen and a QR code, joining on a phone, answering memory questions, the rocket's engines lighting as people submit, and the two colours (the "key") given to each person.
- **Why we made it:** to document the first build and how the flow works.
- **What it led to:** our first round of user testing. Testers didn't realise the prompts were about their teammates, and the host screen felt like leader-and-followers. So we removed Mission Control.
### 10. Paper prototype of a more interactive reveal
 
![Five paper sketches of a laptop screen: a memory with the name blanked, answer choices, a leaderboard, a podium, and finally memory cards around the finished picture](design_artifacts/10-paper-prototype-reveal.png)
 
- **What it shows:** five paper sketches of the reveal on a laptop: a memory with the name blanked out, four answer choices with a live leaderboard, a podium, and finally the memory cards around the finished picture.
- **Why we made it:** our tutor found the reveal too passive, so we sketched a more game-like one that we could test cheaply on paper.
- **What it led to:** our Guess Who reveal. In the final build, the group does a shared activity first (the shared spaceship) and then swipes through memory cards with live reactions.
### 11. UI review of the no-host build
 
![Collage of screens from the no-host version: choosing an astronaut, joining a crew, memory capsules, the privacy card, the rescue mission choice, and the opened capsule](design_artifacts/11-ui-review-no-host-build.png)
 
- **What it shows:** our review of the screens in the version with no host, from choosing an astronaut and joining a crew to the memory capsules, the privacy card, the "memories are lost in space" choice, and the opened capsule.
- **Why we made it:** to go through each screen as a team and spot problems.
- **What it led to:** the changes before our final build, including dropping the colour game and sharing one ship across the crew.
