# Tradeshow Feedback

At the Week 12 tradeshow, students from Teams 6 to 11 filled in the peer feedback form after seeing our pitch, poster and demo. About 22 people gave feedback. This is a summary of what they said. We've left out names and student numbers.

## What people liked

Most reviewers picked up on the main idea of collecting memories separately over time and only opening them together. A few said it made nostalgia feel like something you do with friends rather than a personal photo archive, and that it suits uni friends who are about to move away after graduating.

The shared controls came up a lot. People liked that no one can fly the rocket alone, so getting the memories back actually depends on working together. One person said the game would work as an icebreaker for friends who haven't seen each other in years.

The poster and pitch were described as clear and easy to follow, especially the Collect → Keep → Fly Together → Reveal flow and the space theme.

Several reviewers also noticed that our research and testing changed the design: interviews, thematic analysis, our rounds of testing, refocusing prompts on crewmates, dropping the host role, and switching from separate ships to shared controls.

## What people raised

**Someone missing at the reveal.** This was the biggest one, mentioned by five of the six teams. Since everyone has to be there to unlock the capsule, what happens years later if someone has lost touch, lives in a different time zone, has fallen out with the group, or just doesn't want to join? One person could block everyone else, or make the reunion awkward. People suggested some other way to unlock it that's still fair to whoever isn't there.

**Consent and privacy.** Photos and recordings often include people who never agreed to be in the capsule. Anonymous submissions also mean less accountability. Reviewers asked who owns the memories if someone leaves the group, whether they can still see them, and whether deleting their account would remove part of the group's history. One suggestion was to let people approve or hide memories about them before the group sees them.

**Same memory, different feelings.** A photo that's funny to one person might be embarrassing to someone else. Opening the capsule could also be hard if someone has left the group or passed away.

**Not much happens before the reveal.** Most of the social part is at the unlock. People suggested small things during the semester, like replying to or adding to each other's memories, and something after the reveal, like reacting to memories or comparing how each person remembers the same moment.

**Linking research to design more clearly.** A few people wanted a simple "finding → what we changed" table, and more actual quotes from interviews. We've since added this in the [interviews](../../research/interviews/thematic-analysis.md) and [user testing](../README.md) folders.

**What's real vs simulated.** People were fine with skipping ahead years for the demo, but wanted it clearer which parts actually work and which are standing in for the long-term experience. One reviewer pointed out that without a reminder notification, people would probably forget the capsule exists.

**Other smaller comments:**
- let groups customise what goes in the capsule
- some shared-control roles felt less important than others, which made the game less fun for those players
- the project is strong on time but not really on place; capsules could be linked to meaningful locations

## What we'd do next

The most common suggestion was to test it with real friend groups over a longer period, especially situations like someone dropping out, relationships changing, or someone changing their mind about sharing.

Some of this we'd already started thinking about: a tribute option for members who can't make it, letting people be removed from shared photos, and pop-ups explaining storage and privacy when you sign up.

## Note

Team 11's answers talk about a "revision platform" with fuel and learning mechanics, which isn't our project, so they probably reviewed a different team. We only kept their point about letting people approve or hide memories before others see them.
