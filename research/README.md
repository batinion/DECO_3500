## Secondary research

| Paper | What it told us | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Bluck et al. (2005) | Autobiographical memory serves psychological and social needs | Remembering is social, so reminisce together |
| Conway (2005) | Memory is tied to identity | Memories matter to who people are, so treat them with care |
| Wardell & Palombo (2024) | Memories last through emotional and identity relevance, kept in photos and messages | Use photos and messages as the memory content |
| Hirst et al. (2015) | Significant memories stay accessible for at least a decade | A long gap before reunion still works |
| Petrelli & Whittaker (2010) | Digital mementos feel less significant than physical ones | Adding a memory should feel deliberate |
| Petrelli et al. (2009) | Families imagine a time capsule opening as a celebration | Unlocking should be a shared event |
| Odom et al. (2019) | Digital data never visibly ages, and slowness builds anticipation | Show time passing before memories return |
| Olsson et al. (2020) | Asymmetric roles and shared controls make people cooperate | Split the rocket controls between players |
| Grudin (1994) | Group tools fail when effort and benefit are uneven | Everyone contributes before launch |

## Interviews

10 semi-structured interviews with people aged early 20s to 60+ (P1 to P10).

### Key findings

| # | Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Memories are made together, but kept apart.** Cards, tickets and photos sit in personal boxes or phones. | 8 of 10 keep physical keepsakes, almost always for themselves |
| 2 | **Capture starts on the phone.** The first instinct is to take a photo. | Most participants |
| 3 | **Algorithms decide what comes back.** Memories resurface through "On This Day" prompts, not deliberate revisiting. | 3 of 10 |
| 4 | **Painful memories are avoided.** They are kept but not revisited, or left out. | 5 of 10 |
| 5 | **Old memories reconnect friends.** Old photos restart conversations. | 2 of 10 |
| 6 | **Digital memories are fragile.** Photos are deleted for storage and shared albums disappear. | Several participants |
| 7 | **Revisiting is rare.** Keepsakes are looked at every six to twelve months. | Several participants |

## What the evidence means for us

| Interview finding | Secondary research | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| Memories are kept apart (1) | Remembering serves social needs and identity (Bluck et al., 2005; Conway, 2005) | Memories should be shared and revisited together |
| Digital memories are fragile (6) | Digital mementos feel less significant (Petrelli & Whittaker, 2010) | Adding a memory should feel deliberate |
| Algorithms decide what comes back (3) | Slowness builds anticipation (Odom et al., 2019) | The group, not an algorithm, decides when memories return |
| Revisiting is rare (7) | Capsule openings are celebrations (Petrelli et al., 2009) | Make the return an event worth gathering for |
| Old memories reconnect friends (5) | Significant memories last (Hirst et al., 2015) | Memories can be the reason friends reunite |
| Painful memories are avoided (4) | Memories last through emotional relevance (Wardell & Palombo, 2024) | Never resurface memories automatically |

## Design frameworks

Worked through on our [team Miro board](https://miro.com/app/board/uXjVJnJSoGU=/):

1. **Social design canvas:** objective (reunions, nostalgia and memories), mechanism (memories, mementos, gathering), technology's role, people's contribution, social consequences, key assumptions.
2. **Social possibility wheel:** shared objects, traces and awareness are central.
3. **Space-time matrix:** collecting happens at different times and places. Unlocking happens at the same time and place.
4. **Social / technological / spatial / temporal framework:** type of memories, how they are prepared, and what triggers preserving them.

## From insights to design decisions

| Insight | Design decision |
|---|---|
| Memories are kept apart | One shared capsule for the whole group |
| Uneven effort in groups (Grudin, 1994) | Each person adds at least three memories before launch |
| Capsule opening is a celebration (Petrelli et al., 2009) | Unlocking is a shared game, not a button |
| Asymmetric roles drive cooperation (Olsson et al., 2020) | One rocket: one player moves up, one down, one steers, one collects |
| Data doesn't show its age (Odom et al., 2019) | A "time has passed" journey to the moon before the reunion |
| Painful memories are avoided | Memories only appear when the group chooses to unlock |
